Opportunity · why now

Four shifts have made now the right time

There has always been a gap between the way tradies work and the way digital tools are built. But four specific shifts in the last 24 months mean a one-person team can credibly compete with established agencies — and tradies are finally ready to pay for the right solution.

01
AI collapses agency-quality output into one-person execution What took a 5-person agency a month in 2022 — site design, copywriting, automation setup, ad creative — now takes one operator a week with Claude, Midjourney, and modern automation tools. The right founder can ship at agency quality for 1/10th the headcount cost. That cost gap is the moat.
02
Smartphone penetration in NZ + AU tradies is effectively 100% WhatsApp is the default communication channel on every site. The friction of "managing your business from your phone" is gone — what's missing is the platform that actually does it. Most existing tools are still desktop-first or built for office workers, not for someone covered in concrete dust at 4pm.
03
Google's Local Pack now rewards activity, not just legacy Five years ago, an old plumber with 50 stale reviews would outrank a younger, more active competitor. Today, Google heavily weights review velocity, post frequency, photo updates, and Q&A activity. Every dormant Google Business Profile is a competitor's opportunity. Tradies are losing rankings — and don't know why.
04
Post-COVID labour shortage = tradies are at peak revenue and capacity-constrained Across NZ + AU, building consents and renovations have rebounded. The good tradies have more work than they can handle — but they're losing 12–18 hours per month to admin and quoting, which directly converts to ~$50K/year of foregone billable time. Their bottleneck isn't demand. It's operations. That's exactly what we solve.

02
The problem · quantified

The cost of being "great on the tools, drowning everywhere else"

Most tradies are world-class craftsmen and the worst-served small-business segment when it comes to digital. They didn't pick up the tools to learn Google algorithm updates. They want to be on site — so anything online genuinely struggles, and three specific failures cost them work every single week.

01
No website — or a website built in 2016 The vast majority of NZ + AU tradies either have no website at all or one so dated and slow that it actively works against them. When a homeowner searches "plumber Wellington" at 9pm with a burst pipe, the tradie isn't there to be found. Word of mouth alone caps revenue at whatever the local network gossip can carry. Every job won by a competitor with a half-decent website is a job the better tradie never even knew existed.
02
Google Business Profile is a graveyard — no reviews, no posts, no photos Competitor has 80+ reviews. Tradie has 6. Competitor posts 3x a week. Tradie hasn't posted since 2022. Competitor has answered every question in the Q&A. Tradie's profile has 4 unanswered questions sitting there for two years. When a potential customer DOES find them, the profile looks abandoned. Customers infer "cowboy" or "out of business" and just call the next one on the list. Credibility is binary online — and an unoptimized profile fails the test in two seconds.
03
Social media looks dormant — they look out of business A homeowner doing due diligence on a $30K bathroom job is going to check the tradie's Facebook or Instagram. Last post: December 2022. Three photos from 2019. Their automatic conclusion: "this business has folded — call someone else." The tradie isn't out of business — they're flat-out on jobs and have no time, no system, and no idea what to post. The lost customer never tells them they bounced.
04
Missed call = lost job. No auto-response system A customer rings at 11:30am while the tradie's on a roof. Voicemail. No callback for 4 hours. By then they've called two more tradies and booked the one who answered first. The 60-second auto-text — "Sorry I missed you, on a job — what's the address?" — captures more revenue than any ad campaign. Almost no tradies have it.
05
Quoting at 9pm. Quotes sent days late. Or never The tradie is on tools 7am–5pm. Then quoting at the kitchen table until 9pm. By the time they've typed up Wednesday's quote, the customer's already booked another sparkie. Faster-quoting competitors win the same jobs. No template, no follow-up automation, no record of which quotes are sitting unread. Money walks past every week.
06
Invoicing is a kitchen-table mess. Cash flow suffers Invoices typed in Word, emailed sporadically, paid whenever the customer remembers. No automated payment reminders. Receipts shoved into a shoebox until tax time. Average tradie carries 30–60 days of unpaid invoices they could collect in a week if the system chased it for them. Cash flow problems aren't a revenue problem — they're an admin problem.
07
Reviews happen by accident — if at all The tradie does great work. Customer is thrilled. Review never gets asked for, never gets left. Three months later the same customer can't even remember the tradie's name. Meanwhile the competitor with an automated "thanks for choosing us — leave a review here" SMS goes from 8 reviews to 80 in 12 months. Reviews compound. Stale profiles decay.

"Tradies aren't tech-savvy and have no time to learn. That's a problem for them — and the entire reason this opportunity exists. The same thing that makes them bad at digital makes them desperate for the right partner to run it for them."

— The structural opportunity

Team · operating model

Solo today. AI-augmented. Built to stay lean

The single biggest mistake we could make is hiring before revenue justifies it. The whole point of this business is to keep great tradies on the tools — and the founder applies the same principle to himself. Stay lean, ship at quality, scale the team only when monthly recurring revenue covers it twice over.

How a solo operator delivers at agency quality:

Every install is 80% pre-built and 20% custom. Site templates, automation workflows, email sequences, and lead-qualification logic are all built once and re-configured per client. The 20% that's custom — brand, copy, photos, suburb-specific SEO — is exactly the work that AI accelerates from days to hours.

Founder strategy · the AU + PM-role move

The PM role is not a hedge — it's the GTM channel

The single most important strategic decision in TradieBack's first 18 months is the founder's planned move to Australia and re-entry into a Project Manager role at a major NZ or AU developer. This isn't a contingency — it's the central GTM and capital strategy.

The move: mid-to-late 2026, founder relocates to Australia. Target — a Project Manager role at a tier-1 NZ or AU developer (Sydney/Brisbane or a Christchurch rebuild). Market salary ~$140K–$190K NZD.

Why this isn't a hedge — four reasons:

1 · GTM channel. A PM coordinates 30+ subcontractor trade businesses per week. Every one is a potential client. More qualified than any cold outreach.

2 · Investor pressure removed. A bootstrapped founder with $150K+ of personal runway holds pricing standards and says no to bad-fit clients. Tradies smell desperation in five seconds — the PM income guarantees we never have to project it.

3 · Founder can't be on a computer 24/7. Construction body, construction brain. Best thinking happens on site, not at a desk. Keeps founder in the world he's selling into. Prevents burnout that kills 70% of bootstrapped founders in year one.

4 · AU is 8x the NZ market. Being physically in Australia for 12–18 months builds the relationships and local knowledge needed to scale into the larger of our two markets. NZ proves it. AU scales it.

The trigger to go full-time: TradieBack MRR exceeds PM salary on a sustained 3-month basis. Conservative projection: month 18–24.

"The smartest founders don't bootstrap with desperation. They bootstrap with structural runway — built into the GTM channel."
